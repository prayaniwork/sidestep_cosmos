Clay Playground

Soft, squishy 3D toys in pastel. Tapping things makes them bounce and change colour. Warm and low-pressure, closer to a game you'd happily open every morning than a productivity tool.

Proposal · needs your OK

The loop: from drills to a real idea, every day

Right now each drill ends in a note and nothing happens next. This loop gives every session a goal, a finished thing at the end, and a clear stopping point.

  1. Today's brief

    One small real problem, the same for everyone that day. For example, "Make waiting at the doctor's less awful."

  2. Warm-up

    Three quick drills, tuned to the brief. Second Life hands you an object from a waiting room, and Bridge pairs "a clinic" with something random. Every answer becomes a spark.

  3. Forge

    Pick 2–3 sparks and smash them together in 3D. This is the wow moment. Add one hook from your Why I Stopped notes so the idea grabs people.

  4. Shape it

    Three plain lines: what it's called, who it's for, how it works. Out comes an idea card you can share.

  5. Place it

    The idea joins your 3D world, and you're done for the day. It takes about 10 minutes, with no endless feed.

Why people come back

  • Your world grows. Every idea adds something permanent you can see and explore. Day 30 looks nothing like day 1.
  • One brief a day, like Wordle. It's new every morning, finished in minutes, and easy to compare with friends.
  • Four skill rings that fill as you play: Range (Far Apart), Reframe (Second Life), Connect (Bridge) and Insight (Why I Stopped).
  • Unlocks. New lenses, object packs and rare pairings arrive as you level up.
  • Sunday gallery. Pick your best idea of the week and it becomes a poster you can share.

How notes turn into ideas

  • Sparks are the raw answers from drills. They're the ingredients, not the output.
  • Hooks are your Why I Stopped notes. Log them any time you notice something in the wild. In the Forge they become the part of an idea that grabs people.
  • Ideas are forged from sparks plus a hook. That's the real output, and each one has a name, an audience and a mechanism.
  • Your sketchbook becomes your world: ideas you can revisit, remix, or forge again with new sparks.
